\subsection{State Occupancy Equations}
\label{sec:s2}
State occupancy at each monthly cycle is determined by the partitioned survival model (PSM) approach, using three parametric survival functions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RFS(t)} --- Recurrence-free survival: probability of remaining alive and free of any recurrence (locoregional or distant) at time $t$.
    \item \textbf{DMFS(t)} --- Distant metastasis-free survival: probability of remaining alive and free of distant metastasis at time $t$.
    \item \textbf{OS(t)} --- Overall survival: probability of being alive at time $t$.
\end{itemize}

The occupancy equations are derived from the ordering of these three events:
\begin{itemize}
    \item All patients who are recurrence-free are also distant metastasis-free and alive: $RF = RFS$.
    \item Patients who have had a locoregional recurrence but have not yet developed distant metastasis are those who are distant metastasis-free minus those who are recurrence-free: $LR = DMFS - RFS$.
    \item Patients who are alive with distant metastasis are those who are alive minus those who are distant metastasis-free: $DM = OS - DMFS$.
    \item The dead state is the complement of overall survival: $Dead = 1 - OS$.
\end{itemize}

The constraint $RFS \leq DMFS \leq OS$ is enforced at all time points to ensure non-negative state occupancy. This ordering is clinically meaningful: a patient cannot have distant metastasis without having had a recurrence event (RFS $\leq$ DMFS), and a patient cannot die without having both events (DMFS $\leq$ OS). The ratio-constrained structure $r_{\text{LR}} = RFS/DMFS$ and $r_{\text{DM}} = DMFS/OS$ guarantees this ordering by construction.

\subsection{Observed versus Fitted Landmark Probabilities}
\label{sec:obs_fitted}

Table~\ref{tab:s15} reports the base-case log-normal model predictions at each KEYNOTE-942 landmark time point, together with residuals (fitted minus observed). The mean absolute error (MAE) across all 27 landmark points is 1.98 percentage points, and the root mean squared error (RMSE) is 2.79 percentage points.

The largest residual is at the pembrolizumab-arm RFS 60-month landmark (observed 49.1\%, fitted 40.9\%, residual $-8.2$ percentage points). This reflects two structural features of the model: (i) the ratio-based construction fits $r_{\text{LR}} = \text{RFS}/\text{DMFS}$ as a separate parametric curve, so residuals at the RFS level compound errors from both the DMFS and $r_{\text{LR}}$ components; and (ii) the observed pembrolizumab RFS is identical at 48 and 60 months (both 49.1\%), creating a flat plateau that no standard parametric survival distribution can reproduce---this plateau may reflect sparse event accumulation and sampling variability in the small pembrolizumab arm ($n=52$). The DMFS pembrolizumab 48-month landmark also shows a notable residual ($-5.6$ percentage points) for the same reason.

Despite theselocal deviations, the model provides a reasonable parametric representation of the overall survival trajectory: 22 of 27 landmarks have absolute residuals below 3 percentage points, and the combo-arm curves (which drive the incremental survival benefit and hence the ICER) are well-fitted across all time points (maximum absolute residual 1.5 percentage points for RFS, 1.4 percentage points for DMFS, 0.7 percentage points for OS). The extrapolated long-term survival predictions are consistent with external trial evidence (Section~\ref{sec:external}) and general population mortality constraints (Section~\ref{sec:gp_impl}).

\subsection{GP Mortality Implementation}
\label{sec:gp_impl}
The GP mortality hazard floor is applied to modeled OS from month 60 onward.

\textbf{Life table source:} US 2019 period life tables (Arias et al., NVSS).
\textbf{Baseline age:} 61 years (KEYNOTE-942 median). \textbf{Sex weighting:} 64\% male, 36\% female.
\textbf{Interpolation:} Annual life table survival linearly interpolated to monthly resolution; monthly hazards derived as $h_{\text{GP}}(t) = -\ln[S_{\text{GP}}(t{+}1)/S_{\text{GP}}(t)]$.
\textbf{Constraint formula (mortality hazard floor):} from month 60 onward, the model mortality hazard is floored at the age- and sex-matched general-population hazard:
\begin{equation}
    h_{\text{model}}^{\text{con}}(t) = \max\left[h_{\text{param}}(t),\; h_{\text{GP}}(t)\right] \qquad t \geq 60 \text{ months},
\end{equation}
where $h_{\text{param}}(t) = -\ln[S_{\text{param}}(t{+}1)/S_{\text{param}}(t)]$ is the monthly hazard implied by the fitted parametric survival curve. The constrained survival is reconstructed as $S_{\text{model}}^{\text{con}}(t) = \exp[-\sum_{0}^{t} h^{\text{con}}(u)]$. This is a \textbf{hazard floor}, not a survival cap: a cohort that has already experienced elevated (cancer-related) mortality in the first 5 years remains below general-population survival at every subsequent time point, whereas a survival cap ($S = \min[S_{\text{param}}, S_{\text{GP}}]$) would incorrectly force the two curves to coincide whenever the parametric tail exceeded the general-population level. Both arms are subject to the same background mortality floor.

\subsection{Probabilistic Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:psa}

\subsubsection{PSA Implementation Details}
PSA with 6,000 Monte Carlo iterations (seed = 42). Survival parameters ($\mu$, $\sigma$ for 6 curves) drawn jointly from bivariate normal via Jacobian covariance ($r > 0.95$); $\sigma$ lower-truncated at 0.05. Costs: gamma (CV=0.2). Utilities: beta (SE=0.03). Structural constraints (RFS $\leq$ DMFS $\leq$ OS, monotonicity, survival within [0,1]) verified every cycle; all 6,000 draws were structurally valid (0 rejections). No iterations were excluded on the basis of the ICER value.

\noindent\textbf{Note on boundary solutions:} Two $\sigma$ parameters ($r_{\text{DM}}$--pembrolizumab and $r_{\text{LR}}$--combo) reach the upper bound of the estimation window (Table~\ref{tab:s12}). This occurs because the corresponding ratio curves are approximately constant over the observed time points (pembrolizumab $r_{\text{DM}}$: 0.78, 0.76, 0.76, 0.76; combo $r_{\text{LR}}$: 0.87, 0.86, 0.86, 0.86, 0.82), and a log-normal distribution with very large $\sigma$ approximates a constant function. The Jacobian-based covariance matrices for these two parameter pairs are therefore approximate, as the true Fisher information matrix is not well-defined at a boundary solution. In PSA, samples are drawn from a bivariate normal distribution truncated at the upper bound; this truncation is conservative in that it limits the sampled $\sigma$ values to the estimation window, preventing extreme tail behavior that could produce structurally invalid survival curves. Sensitivity analyses using alternative distributions (Weibull, log-logistic; Supplementary Table~S5) yield ICERs in the range of $\$40,000$--$\$60,000$/QALY, suggesting that the cost-effectiveness conclusion is robust to the choice of distribution specification for these components.

\subsection{Structural Scenario Analyses}
\label{sec:scenarios}

\noindent\textbf{Scenario implementations.} Three structural scenarios alter the base-case treatment-effect assumptions. All scenarios preserve the observed 5-year KEYNOTE-942 data unchanged and modify only the extrapolated trajectory beyond 60 months.

\textit{OS-survival convergence (5\textendash20\,y, Waning V1).} On the \textbf{survival probability scale}, the incremental OS benefit (combo OS minus pembrolizumab OS) is linearly attenuated from its full base-case value at 60 months to zero at 240 months (20 years). At each monthly cycle $t$: $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = S_{\text{pembro}}(t) + [S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{base}}(t) - S_{\text{pembro}}(t)] \times \max\bigl(0,\; 1 - \frac{t - 60}{180}\bigr)$. The recurrence ($r_{\text{DM}}$) and local-recurrence ($r_{\text{LR}}$) ratio functions are \textbf{not} modified, so the scenario isolates the effect of attenuating the OS benefit while preserving the observed recurrence advantage. The general-population hazard floor continues to apply independently to both arms.

\textit{Hazard convergence (5\textendash10\,y, Waning V2).} On the \textbf{cause-specific mortality hazard scale}, the combo arm's monthly all-cause hazard is linearly blended toward the pembrolizumab arm's hazard from month 60 to month 120 (10 years). At each cycle: $h_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = (1 - w(t))\,h_{\text{combo}}(t) + w(t)\,h_{\text{pembro}}(t)$, where $w(t) = \max\bigl(0,\; \min\bigl(\frac{t - 60}{60},\; 1\bigr)\bigr)$. The waned hazard is integrated forward to reconstruct the survival curve: $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t+1) = S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) \times \exp\bigl(-h_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t)\bigr)$, with $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{base}}(t)$ for $t \leq 60$ months. In this scenario, $r_{\text{DM}}$ and $r_{\text{LR}}$ are also linearly converged to pembrolizumab-arm values on the probability scale (HR\,$\to$\,1 by 10 years), so that all three survival components reflect complete treatment-effect erosion. The GP hazard floor continues to apply.

\textit{No direct OS benefit.} This counterfactual scenario assigns zero direct OS treatment effect to intismeran: the combination arm's OS curve is set equal to the pembrolizumab arm's OS curve at all time points ($S_{\text{combo}}(t) = S_{\text{pembro}}(t)\;\forall\;t$). The recurrence ($r_{\text{DM}}$) and local-recurrence ($r_{\text{LR}}$) ratio functions retain their base-case values, so any incremental QALY gain arises solely from the improved disease-state trajectory (longer recurrence-free and distant-metastasis-free intervals) rather than from extended overall survival. RFS\,$\leq$\,DMFS\,$\leq$\,OS ordering is maintained trivially since both arms share the same OS curve.

\subsection{Technical Reproducibility}
\label{sec:tech_repro}
All analyses were performed using Python 3.12.7 (Apple Clang 15.0.0) on macOS 26.6.2 (Apple Silicon). The full code repository is available at \url{https://github.com/yichao2022/cea-intismeran} (tag \texttt{v1.0-submission}).

\textbf{Key dependencies:} NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.14.1, Matplotlib 3.9.3, Pandas 2.2.3, LaTeX (TeX Live 2025, pdfLaTeX + BibTeX + natbib).

\textbf{Model details:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cycle length: monthly (1/12 year); time horizon: 40 years (480 cycles)
    \item Half-cycle correction: not applied (monthly cycles are sufficiently fine-grained)
    \item Discounting: continuous-time exponential: $1/(1 + r)^t$ where $r = 0.03$
    \item Random seed: 42 (PSA reproducibility)
    \item GP mortality source: US 2019 period life tables (NVSS, Arias et al. 2022)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Code structure:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{model.py} --- ModelParams, CEAModel (4-state PSM)
    \item \texttt{rerun\_primary.py} --- CEAModelV2 (hazard-based GP floor, waning, no-OS)
    \item \texttt{psa\_full.py} --- PSA with joint survival sampling
    \item \texttt{survival\_model\_selection.py} --- 15,625-combination model selection
    \item \texttt{final\_run.py} / \texttt{threshold\_analysis.py} --- DSA, scenarios, pricing
    \item \texttt{figures.py} / \texttt{gen\_fig\_*.py} --- Figure generation scripts
    \item \texttt{output/survival\_combos\_15625.csv} --- Full model selection results
\end{itemize}
