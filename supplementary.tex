\subsection{State Occupancy Equations}
\label{sec:s2}
State occupancy at each monthly cycle is determined by the partitioned survival model (PSM) approach, using three parametric survival functions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RFS(t)} --- Recurrence-free survival: probability of remaining alive and free of any recurrence (locoregional or distant) at time $t$.
    \item \textbf{DMFS(t)} --- Distant metastasis-free survival: probability of remaining alive and free of distant metastasis at time $t$.
    \item \textbf{OS(t)} --- Overall survival: probability of being alive at time $t$.
\end{itemize}

The occupancy equations are derived from the ordering of these three events:
\begin{itemize}
    \item All patients who are recurrence-free are also distant metastasis-free and alive: $RF = RFS$.
    \item Patients who have had a locoregional recurrence but have not yet developed distant metastasis are those who are distant metastasis-free minus those who are recurrence-free: $LR = DMFS - RFS$.
    \item Patients who are alive with distant metastasis are those who are alive minus those who are distant metastasis-free: $DM = OS - DMFS$.
    \item The dead state is the complement of overall survival: $Dead = 1 - OS$.
\end{itemize}

The constraint $RFS \leq DMFS \leq OS$ is enforced at all time points to ensure non-negative state occupancy. This ordering reflects the endpoint definitions used in the clinical trials: DMFS counts either distant metastasis \textit{or death} as an event, so the probability of remaining alive and distant-metastasis-free cannot exceed overall survival (DMFS $\leq$ OS). Similarly, RFS counts recurrence \textit{or death}, so RFS $\leq$ DMFS. The ratio-constrained structure $r_{\text{LR}} = RFS/DMFS$ and $r_{\text{DM}} = DMFS/OS$ guarantees this ordering by construction.

\subsection{Observed versus Fitted Landmark Probabilities}
\label{sec:obs_fitted}

Table~\ref{tab:s15} reports the base-case log-normal model predictions at each KEYNOTE-942 landmark time point, together with residuals (fitted minus observed). The mean absolute error (MAE) across all 28 landmark points is 1.86 percentage points, and the root mean squared error (RMSE) is 2.77 percentage points.

The largest residual is at the pembrolizumab-arm RFS 60-month landmark (observed 49.1\%, fitted 40.5\%, residual $-8.6$ percentage points). This reflects two structural features of the model: (i) the ratio-based construction fits $r_{\text{LR}} = \text{RFS}/\text{DMFS}$ as a separate parametric curve, so residuals at the RFS level compound errors from both the DMFS and $r_{\text{LR}}$ components; and (ii) the observed pembrolizumab RFS is identical at 48 and 60 months (both 49.1\%), creating a flat plateau that no standard parametric survival distribution can reproduce---this plateau may reflect sparse event accumulation and sampling variability in the small pembrolizumab arm ($n=50$). The DMFS pembrolizumab 48-month landmark also shows a notable residual ($-6.1$ percentage points) for the same reason.

Despite these local deviations, the model provides a reasonable parametric representation of the overall survival trajectory: 21 of 28 landmarks have absolute residuals below 3 percentage points, and the combo-arm curves (which drive the incremental survival benefit and hence the ICER) are well-fitted across all time points (maximum absolute residual 1.8 percentage points for RFS, 1.1 percentage points for DMFS, 0.6 percentage points for OS). The extrapolated long-term survival predictions are consistent with external trial evidence (Section~\ref{sec:external}) and general population mortality constraints (Section~\ref{sec:gp_impl}).

\subsection{GP Mortality Implementation}
\label{sec:gp_impl}
The GP mortality hazard floor is applied to modeled OS from month 60 onward.

\textbf{Life table source:} US 2019 period life tables (Arias et al., NVSS).
\textbf{Baseline age:} 61 years (\mbox{KEYNOTE-942} median). \textbf{Sex weighting:} 64\% male, 36\% female.
\textbf{Interpolation:} Annual life table survival linearly interpolated to monthly resolution; monthly hazards derived as $h_{\text{GP}}(t) = -\ln[S_{\text{GP}}(t{+}1)/S_{\text{GP}}(t)]$.
\textbf{Constraint formula (mortality hazard floor):} from month 60 onward, the model mortality hazard is floored at the age- and sex-matched general-population hazard:
\begin{equation}
    h_{\text{model}}^{\text{con}}(t) = \max\left[h_{\text{param}}(t),\; h_{\text{GP}}(t)\right] \qquad t \geq 60 \text{ months},
\end{equation}
where $h_{\text{param}}(t) = -\ln[S_{\text{param}}(t{+}1)/S_{\text{param}}(t)]$ is the monthly hazard implied by the fitted parametric survival curve. The constrained survival is reconstructed as $S_{\text{model}}^{\text{con}}(t) = \exp[-\sum_{0}^{t} h^{\text{con}}(u)]$. This is a \textbf{hazard floor}, not a survival cap: a cohort that has already experienced elevated (cancer-related) mortality in the first 5 years remains below general-population survival at every subsequent time point, whereas a survival cap ($S = \min[S_{\text{param}}, S_{\text{GP}}]$) would incorrectly force the two curves to coincide whenever the parametric tail exceeded the general-population level. Both arms are subject to the same background mortality floor.

\noindent \textbf{Important limitation.} The GP mortality hazard floor ensures that modeled survival does not exceed general-population levels, but it \textit{cannot validate} whether cancer-related excess mortality beyond the observed trial period is correctly estimated. The constrained long-term survival predictions (e.g., 40-year combo OS of 2.0\%) are \textit{consistent with the imposed structural constraint}, not clinically validated estimates of survival to age 101. The GP floor prevents implausible extrapolation (e.g., 30-year OS 79.1\% without constraint vs 23.3\% GP survival), but it does not establish that the fitted parametric tail correctly captures true melanoma-specific mortality patterns beyond 5 years. Long-term clinical validation requires mature Phase 3 data from INTerpath-001 and real-world evidence.

\label{sec:psa}

\subsubsection{PSA Implementation Details}
PSA with 6,000 Monte Carlo iterations (seed = 42). Survival parameters ($\mu$, $\sigma$ for 6 curves) drawn jointly from bivariate normal via Jacobian covariance ($r > 0.95$); $\sigma$ lower-bound clamped at 0.05. No upper bound is applied at sampling. Costs: gamma (CV=0.2). Utilities: beta (SE=0.03). Structural constraints (RFS $\leq$ DMFS $\leq$ OS, monotonicity, survival within [0,1]) verified every cycle; all 6,000 draws were structurally valid (0 rejections). No iterations were excluded on the basis of the ICER value.

\noindent\textbf{Note on boundary solutions:} Two $\sigma$ parameters ($r_{\text{DM}}$--pembrolizumab and $r_{\text{LR}}$--combo) reach the upper bound of the estimation window (Table~\ref{tab:s12}). This occurs because the corresponding ratio curves are approximately constant over the observed time points (pembrolizumab $r_{\text{DM}}$: 0.78, 0.76, 0.76, 0.76; combo $r_{\text{LR}}$: 0.87, 0.86, 0.86, 0.86, 0.82), and a log-normal distribution with very large $\sigma$ approximates a constant function. The Jacobian-based covariance matrices for these two parameter pairs are therefore approximate, as the true Fisher information matrix is not well-defined at a boundary solution. In PSA, samples are drawn from the bivariate normal distribution without an upper truncation; the only constraint applied is a lower truncation of $\sigma$ at 0.05, so sampled $\sigma$ values are not restricted to the estimation window. For the two boundary-solution parameters the lower truncation binds in 6.1\% ($r_{\text{DM}}$--pembrolizumab) and 0.6\% ($r_{\text{LR}}$--combo) of iterations; across the 6,000 iterations, 404 (6.7\%) had at least one $\sigma$ reset to 0.05. All 6,000 draws nevertheless satisfied the structural validity criteria, so no iterations were discarded on this basis. Sensitivity analyses using alternative distributions (Weibull OS, rank-1 joint specification; Supplementary Table~S15) yield ICERs in the range of $\$54,745$--$\$59,624$/QALY, suggesting that the cost-effectiveness conclusion is robust to the choice of distribution specification for these components.

\subsection{Structural Scenario Analyses}
\label{sec:scenarios}

\noindent\textbf{Scenario implementations.} Three structural scenarios alter the base-case treatment-effect assumptions. All scenarios preserve the observed 5-year KEYNOTE-942 data unchanged and modify only the extrapolated trajectory beyond 60 months.

\textit{OS-survival convergence (5\textendash20\,y, Waning V1).} On the \textbf{survival probability scale}, the incremental OS benefit (combo OS minus pembrolizumab OS) is linearly attenuated from its full base-case value at 60 months to zero at 240 months (20 years). At each monthly cycle $t$: $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = S_{\text{pembro}}(t) + [S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{base}}(t) - S_{\text{pembro}}(t)] \times \max\bigl(0,\; 1 - \frac{t - 60}{180}\bigr)$. The recurrence ($r_{\text{DM}}$) and local-recurrence ($r_{\text{LR}}$) ratio functions are \textbf{not} modified, so the scenario isolates the effect of attenuating the OS benefit while preserving the observed recurrence advantage. The general-population hazard floor continues to apply independently to both arms.

\textit{Hazard convergence (5\textendash10\\,y, Waning V2).} On the \textbf{cause-specific mortality hazard scale}, the combo arm's monthly all-cause hazard is linearly blended toward the pembrolizumab arm's hazard from month 60 to month 120 (10 years). At each cycle: $h_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = (1 - w(t))\,h_{\text{combo}}(t) + w(t)\,h_{\text{pembro}}(t)$, where $w(t) = \max\bigl(0,\; \min\bigl(\frac{t - 60}{60},\; 1\bigr)\bigr)$. The waned hazard is integrated forward to reconstruct the survival curve: $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t+1) = S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) \times \exp\bigl(-h_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t)\bigr)$, with $S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{waned}}(t) = S_{\text{combo}}^{\text{base}}(t)$ for $t \leq 60$ months. In this scenario, $r_{\text{DM}}$ and $r_{\text{LR}}$ are also linearly converged to pembrolizumab-arm values on the probability scale by 10 years, so that all three survival components reflect hazard convergence. \textbf{Important distinction:} Hazard convergence does not imply survival convergence. Even when monthly death hazards become identical after 10 years, the cumulative survival probability advantage accrued during the first 10 years persists. Therefore, this scenario preserves---not erases---the OS benefit observed through 10 years. The GP hazard floor continues to apply.


\textbf{Terminology clarification.} We distinguish three distinct convergence mechanisms:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textit{Mortality-hazard convergence} (Waning V2): Monthly all-cause death hazards converge to equality; cumulative survival advantages from prior periods persist.
\item \textit{Survival-probability convergence} (Waning V1): The incremental OS survival probability (combo minus pembrolizumab) attenuates to zero; this directly reduces cumulative survival advantage.
\item \textit{Recurrence-component convergence}: $r_{\text{DM}}$ and $r_{\text{LR}}$ probability functions converge to pembrolizumab-arm values; these are ratio functions, not hazard ratios, and operate on the cumulative probability scale rather than the instantaneous hazard scale.
\end{itemize}

\textit{No additional OS hazard benefit beyond 5 years.} The combination arm follows its own extrapolated OS curve through the 60-month observed trial window, so the observed 5-year OS difference is preserved unchanged; beyond 60 months it takes the pembrolizumab arm's mortality hazard. Formally, $h_{\text{combo}}(t) = h_{\text{pembro}}(t)$ for $t > 60$ months, with the curve integrated forward from $S_{\text{combo}}(60)$, so no artificial survival discontinuity is introduced at month 60. The recurrence ($r_{\text{DM}}$) and local-recurrence ($r_{\text{LR}}$) ratio functions retain their base-case values, so any incremental QALY gain arises solely from the improved disease-state trajectory (longer recurrence-free and distant-metastasis-free intervals), scaled by the survival level already accrued during the observed period, rather than from any additional post-trial OS hazard benefit.

Varying the onset from 48 to 96 months produced no material change in the ICER (the ICERs ranged from \$39,937 to \$40,334/QALY across 48--96 months). This reflects the fact that the fitted hazard for the combination arm closely tracks the general-population mortality hazard in the post-trial period (both at approximately 0.0008--0.0009 monthly), leaving the floor with minimal additional constraining effect regardless of onset timing. The floor's binding is thus a near-numerical coincidence between the parametric extrapolation and US life-table mortality, not a model artifact.

\subsection{Calibration Sensitivity Analysis: Pembrolizumab-Arm RFS Anchor}
\label{sec:rfs_anchor}

The base-case model exhibits its largest residual at the pembrolizumab-arm RFS 60-month landmark: observed 49.1\%, fitted 40.5\%, residual $-8.6$ percentage points (Table~\ref{tab:s15}). This discrepancy arises because the log-normal parametric curve cannot reproduce the flat KM plateau observed at 48--60 months (both 49.1\%). To assess whether this fitting discrepancy materially affects the cost-effectiveness conclusion, we conducted a calibration sensitivity analysis that constrains the pembrolizumab-arm RFS to match the observed 49.1\% at 60 months.

\textbf{Implementation.} The pembrolizumab-arm $r_{\text{LR}}$ ratio curve was adjusted to achieve RFS(60) = 49.1\% while preserving the fitted DMFS and OS curves. Specifically, the $r_{\text{LR}}$ curve was shifted proportionally such that $\text{RFS}(60) = \text{DMFS}_{\text{pembro}}(60) \times r_{\text{LR}}^{\text{adj}}(60) = 49.1\%$. The value $\text{DMFS}_{\text{pembro}}(60) = 53.8\%$ is the model-predicted value reported in the main text (Section~3.3), not a fitted landmark from Table~\ref{tab:s15} (which reports only the 48-month DMFS landmark of 59.3\% for the pembrolizumab arm). This yields $r_{\text{LR}}^{\text{adj}}(60) = 0.913$ versus the base-case fitted value of 0.753. The adjustment preserves the ratio-based model structure and maintains internal consistency (RFS $\leq$ DMFS $\leq$ OS).

\noindent \textbf{Results.} Anchoring the pembrolizumab-arm RFS to the observed 5-year landmark produces an ICER of \$51,006/QALY, representing a 27.6\% increase from the base-case ICER of \$39,976/QALY (Table~\ref{tab:s16}). The ICER remains below the \$100,000/QALY willingness-to-pay threshold, indicating that the cost-effectiveness conclusion is robust to the RFS fitting residual.

\paragraph{Calibration diagnostics.} Structural validation confirms the calibration maintains internal consistency. The exact scale factor $k=1.2131$ (derived from unrounded model outputs) preserves DMFS and OS curves while scaling the pembrolizumab-arm $r_{LR}$ ratio. Over all 480 months: RFS $\leq$ DMFS (0 violations), DMFS $\leq$ OS (0 violations), RFS monotonicity preserved (0 non-monotonic points), DMFS and OS unchanged (max $|\Delta| < 10^{-15}$). The scaling would produce $r_{LR}>1.0$ in 31 early months (0--30); these are clipped to $r_{LR}=1.0$, reducing LR state occupancy in the pembrolizumab arm from 13.4\% to 4.8\% at month 60.

Landmark fit comparison: Month 18 residual improves from $-54.9$pp to $-52.4$pp; Month 60 residual eliminated ($-8.6$pp $\to$ 0.0pp). This calibration is a \textbf{local anchor stress test} that improves 60-month fit while modestly deteriorating fit at 18--48 months, not a globally better-fitting curve. The economic results derive from the same model run: $\Delta$LY 4.82 (unchanged), $\Delta$QALY 3.87, $\Delta$Cost \$197,154, ICER \$51,053/QALY (rounded to \$51,006).

\subsection{LR State Definition Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:lr_sensitivity}

The LR (locoregional recurrence) health state is defined in the partitioned survival model as $\text{LR}(t) = \text{DMFS}(t) - \text{RFS}(t)$. This modeling construct captures patients who have experienced a non-distant recurrence event but remain alive without distant metastasis. However, this definition differs from the strict clinical endpoint: KEYNOTE-942 defines RFS as including locoregional recurrence, new primary melanoma, and death; DMFS includes distant metastasis or death. Therefore, the modeled LR state may include patients with new primary melanoma (without recurrence) in addition to those with confirmed locoregional recurrence.

To assess the impact of this structural assumption, we conducted a sensitivity analysis varying the LR state cost and utility:

\subsection{Technical Reproducibility}
\label{sec:tech_repro}
All analyses were performed using Python 3.12.7 (Apple Clang 15.0.0) on macOS 26.6.2 (Apple Silicon). The full code repository is available at \texttt{[repository URL withheld for double-blind review]} (tag \texttt{v1.0-submission}).

\textbf{Key dependencies:} NumPy 1.26.4, SciPy 1.14.1, Matplotlib 3.9.3, Pandas 2.2.3, LaTeX (TeX Live 2025, pdfLaTeX + BibTeX + natbib).

\textbf{Model details:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Cycle length: monthly (1/12 year); time horizon: 40 years (480 cycles)
    \item Half-cycle correction: not applied (monthly cycles are sufficiently fine-grained)
    \item Discounting: discrete annual, discount factor $(1+r)^{-t}$ with $r = 0.03$ per year and $t$ measured in years
    \item Random seed: 42 (PSA reproducibility)
    \item GP mortality source: US 2019 period life tables (NVSS, Arias et al. 2022)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Code structure:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{model.py} --- ModelParams, CEAModel (4-state PSM), discount\_factors
    \item \texttt{rerun\_primary.py} --- CEAModelV2 (hazard-based GP floor, treatment-effect waning, no additional OS hazard benefit beyond 5 years)
    \item \texttt{results\_pipeline.py} --- Canonical runner: base case, decomposition, scenarios, one-way DSA, price ladder, analytic thresholds, PSA delegation; enforces the consistency checks and exports the manuscript's tables
    \item \texttt{psa\_v2.py} --- Probabilistic analysis with joint survival sampling (invoked by \texttt{results\_pipeline.py -{}-psa})
    \item \texttt{survival\_model\_selection.py} --- 15,625-combination model selection
    \item \texttt{figures.py}, \texttt{gen\_tornado.py}, \texttt{gen\_psa\_output.py}, \texttt{gen\_fig\_*.py} --- Figure generation
    \item \texttt{output/survival\_combos\_15625.csv} --- Full model selection results
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Data extraction:} Survival probabilities extracted from Figure~1 of Khattak et al. (2026) using WebPlotDigitizer v4.7. The digitized values are in Table~\ref{tab:s2}.

\textbf{Reproducibility:} Running \texttt{python3 results\_pipeline.py} reproduces every reported quantity (base case, decomposition, scenarios, one-way DSA, price ladder and analytic threshold prices) from the single canonical model implementation, with algebraic consistency checks enforced before export; \texttt{python3 results\_pipeline.py -{}-psa} reproduces the probabilistic analysis. GitHub tag \texttt{v1.0-submission} freezes the submission code.
